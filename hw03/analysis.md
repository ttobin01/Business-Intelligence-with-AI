# HW3 Analysis: Do Executive Changes Precede or Follow Earnings?

**Data:** `hw03/corporate_events_timeline.csv`. It holds 30 executive events from 16 Item 5.02 8-K filings dated 2025-09-30 to 2026-07-02, and each event is matched to the nearest of the 20 Item 2.02 earnings filings in `earnings_history.csv`.

Based on this data, executive changes at these five companies mostly *preceded* earnings announcements: 22 of the 30 events were filed before the nearest earnings filing, 6 after, and 2 in the same week. Counting filings rather than people, since one 8-K often reports several changes, 11 of 16 came before earnings, 4 after, and 1 in the same week. The largest changes followed this pattern. Walmart's CEO succession was filed 6 days before its Q3 FY26 release, Apple's CEO transition 10 days before its fiscal Q2 2026 results, and JPMorgan's Co-President promotions 19 days before its Q2 2026 results, which is consistent with companies disclosing leadership news ahead of the earnings call so management can address it there. The pattern does vary by company: Apple, JPMorgan, NVIDIA and Walmart were mostly "before," while two of Microsoft's three events (board changes tied to its annual shareholder meeting cycle) came after earnings. With only 16 filings, the lean toward "before" is suggestive rather than conclusive (a one-sided binomial test of 11 of 15 non-same-week filings against a 50/50 split gives p ≈ 0.06).

*Caveats:* timing is measured from 8-K **filing** dates, not the `effective_date` of each change, and several changes take effect months later (e.g., Apple's CEO transition on 2026-09-01). The earnings table covers only each company's four most recent quarters. I checked the edge of that window: for the earliest events (MSFT 2025-09-30, WMT 2025-10-22), the earlier earnings filing left out of the table is further away than the one matched, so the window does not change any label.
